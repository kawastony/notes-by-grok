## 1. Second-order scalar Π_ij — checked

At second order, the scalar stress contains
\[
T_{ij}^{(2)}\supset\partial_i\delta\phi\,\partial_j\delta\phi-\tfrac13 g_{ij}(\nabla\delta\phi)^2+\cdots

\[
\Pi_{ij}^{(2)}\neq0\quad\text{(formally)}.

\[
\Pi^{(2)}\sim\mathcal{O}(\delta^2)\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{-10})
\]
relative to background density — far below the observational window \(|\eta|\sim0.02\)–0.12.

**Verdict:** The second-order scalar shear exists mathematically and is **cosmologically irrelevant** for linear slip tests. Inside nonlinear collapsed objects it is part of local galactic dynamics (SPARC / a_T sector), not a linear cosmological η signal.

The pasted O(10^{-10}) estimate is consistent with standard perturbation counting. No quantitative η from scalar second order at linear cosmological scales.

## 2. KK / bulk Weyl channel — what is known

In 5D Einstein gravity with a warped bulk, the **projected bulk Weyl tensor**
\[
E_{\mu\nu}\equiv C^{A}{}_{BCD}\,n_A e^B{}_\mu n^C e^D{}_\nu
\]
appears in the effective 4D equations (Shiromizu–Maeda–Sasaki braneworld projection, and analogues for thick/continuous warps).

\(E_{\mu\nu}\) can be decomposed as an effective “Weyl fluid” with energy density, momentum, and **anisotropic stress** \(\pi^E_{\mu\nu}\). That anisotropic stress **can** source
\[
\Phi-\Psi\propto\pi^E
\]
on the 4D slices.

Standard facts:

| Fact | Implication |
|---|---|
| On a pure AdS / unperturbed warped background, \(E_{\mu\nu}=0\) | No background slip from Weyl |
| \(E_{\mu\nu}\) is nonlocal and not closed by 4D equations alone | Needs bulk solution |
| KK graviton modes (massive spin-2) can carry anisotropic stress | In principle a slip channel |
| Zero-mode graviton | Recovers standard 4D GR (no extra slip) |
